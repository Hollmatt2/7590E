# Usability sessions

Required before the managerial storytelling presentation on 10/7 (brief, section 11). Sessions are with people
outside the team; working professionals in procurement, finance, legal or operations are ideal, and classmates
on other teams are acceptable. Record roles, not names. A session that produced no change is still a finding:
say so and explain the decision.

## Plan

**Two sessions, 30 minutes each, by 2026-09-26**, which leaves ten days to act on what they show before 10/7.
A third is worth doing if the first two disagree.

Aim for one person who works with contracts or purchasing (they will react to the review screen) and one who
does not (they will react to the intake form). The brief only asks for people outside the team.

Run it over Zoom with their screen shared, or in person. Record it if they agree, so you are watching rather
than typing.

**Set up before they arrive:** a fresh agreement in the queue for them to review, and a sample contract on
their desktop to upload. The demo logins are on the live site: requester, reviewer, approver.

## The one rule

Give them the goal, not the steps. "You have a contract from a new supplier and you need it reviewed" tells
you what they do; "click Submit an agreement" tells you nothing. When they get stuck, ask what they expected to
happen, then wait. Silence is the tool.

## Tasks

Give these one at a time. Record where they hesitate, what they call things, and what they expect to happen
next.

1. **Submit a contract.** "A new supplier sent this agreement. Your business unit needs it signed by the end of
   the month. Get it reviewed." *(Watch: do they understand needed-by? Do they look for a place to explain why
   it matters? Do they know what happens next?)*
2. **Find out where it stands.** "You are in a meeting and someone asks whether that contract is cleared."
   *(Watch: do they find My submissions? Is the status wording meaningful to them? Do they expect an email?)*
3. **Review a contract.** Log in as the reviewer. "Go through this contract and decide what needs attention."
   *(Watch: do they read the quoted words or the provision name? Do they notice severity? Do they understand
   accept, dismiss and escalate without being told? What do they type in the reason box?)*
4. **Deal with a weak finding.** Point them at a finding marked "check carefully". "What would you do with
   this one?" *(Watch: does the mark change their behaviour, or do they ignore it? This tests the threshold
   decision directly.)*
5. **Answer a question the app does not answer.** "Does this contract let the supplier hand the work to
   someone else?" *(Watch: do they find the search box? Do they trust it? This tests the human fallback.)*
6. **Close it out.** "Record the outcome." *(Watch: do they understand why the outcome is blocked until every
   finding has a decision? Do they know what cleared with conditions means for the requester?)*

## What to watch for, and what it would mean

| If you see | It probably means |
|---|---|
| They read provision names and skip the quoted text | The finding layout leads with the wrong thing |
| They ask "is this bad?" | They expect advice, and the system deliberately does not advise. How that is communicated matters |
| They write one-word reasons | The reason box needs a prompt, or reasons are being treated as a formality |
| They ignore the low-confidence mark | The 0.9 threshold is not earning its place (`docs/threshold-note.md`) |
| They dismiss findings quickly without reading | Precision problem: we are wasting their attention |
| They look for a way to ask the requester a question | The missing feature is the comment thread listed as stretch in section 8 |
| They expect an email when something changes | Notifications are absent by design (ambiguity log, question 1) |
| They cannot tell which agreements need them | The work queue's grouping is wrong for how they actually work |

## Afterwards

Within a day, while it is fresh:

1. Fill in a session record below.
2. Write down every change you decided to make, and every change you decided against with the reason. The
   brief grades the decision, not the length of the list.
3. Put anything you will not do now into the known-issues list, so it is a choice rather than an omission.

## Recruiting message

> I am building a contract-review tool for my capstone at UGA and I need 30 minutes with someone who works
> with contracts or purchasing. You would try it while I watch and say what you expect to happen. No
> preparation, nothing to install, and it is a made-up company, so nothing confidential. Would this week or
> next suit you?
